# Article Quality Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing a new guide or materially updating an existing page. A page
must be useful on its own, accurate, and meaningfully distinct from the site's other URLs.

## Intent and usefulness

- [ ] The page serves a distinct user need and one primary search intent.
- [ ] The title, introduction, headings, and conclusion answer the same need.
- [ ] A direct answer appears near the beginning.
- [ ] The explanation is original and gives the reader useful context beyond a calculator result.
- [ ] Worked examples are practical, transparent, and independently recalculated.
- [ ] Assumptions, units, rounding, scope, and common mistakes are explicit.
- [ ] No word count or FAQ section has been added merely to make the page longer.
- [ ] FAQs, if included, answer real follow-up questions and do not repeat the body.

## Accuracy and evidence

- [ ] Every calculation, conversion factor, and example has been checked independently.
- [ ] Formula inputs, edge cases, and calculator output agree.
- [ ] Factual claims have credible, relevant citations close to the claim or in a clear source list.
- [ ] Sources are accessible, current enough for the claim, and appropriate to the relevant region.
- [ ] Product, legal, financial, health, tax, or construction guidance has the required specialist
  and jurisdiction-specific review.
- [ ] No unsupported demand, performance, competitor, or outcome claims appear.
- [ ] Author and reviewer identities and qualifications are accurate; no credentials are invented.
- [ ] Limitations and uncertainty are clear. Health, legal, tax, and financial pages explain that
  the calculator is informational and not individualized professional advice where appropriate.

## Site fit and technical details

- [ ] Links point to real, relevant calculators and related guides; no planned or missing route is
  presented as live.
- [ ] Internal links use the canonical URL and help the reader continue their task.
- [ ] The title and meta description accurately summarize the page without keyword stuffing.
- [ ] Heading structure is descriptive and navigable; headings are not repeated for search phrases.
- [ ] Canonical points to the intended URL; robots directives match the review decision.
- [ ] Only approved, reviewed pages are indexable and included in the sitemap.
- [ ] The page renders correctly on mobile and the calculator works with keyboard and assistive
  technology.
- [ ] Structured data, if used, matches visible content. FAQ markup is not treated as a guarantee
  of a Google rich result.

## Search quality and overlap

- [ ] Existing guides, calculator pages, route inventory, keyword rows, and intent clusters were
  checked before proposing a URL.
- [ ] The page does not duplicate or lightly reword a useful existing page.
- [ ] Related intents are merged into the best existing URL when that is clearer for users.
- [ ] No spun content, mass-produced near-duplicates, fake data, misleading claims, or superficial
  date changes are present.
- [ ] Keywords are used naturally; no keyword stuffing or unsupported volume/CPC/difficulty claims
  are present.

## Final editorial decision

Choose exactly one and record the reason:

- [ ] **Publish** — distinct, accurate, sourced, reviewed, and technically ready.
- [ ] **Merge into an existing page** — the intent is already served or is a better fit there.
- [ ] **Revise** — useful opportunity, but evidence, accuracy, or UX is not ready.
- [ ] **Reject** — insufficient user value, evidence, differentiation, or safe scope.

Record the decision, owner, factual reviewer, date, source links, and any unresolved review items.
